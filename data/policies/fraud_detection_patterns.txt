MEDICARE FRAUD, WASTE, AND ABUSE DETECTION PATTERNS

1. COMMON FRAUD SCHEMES IN MEDICARE CLAIMS

A. UPCODING
Definition: Billing for a higher level of service than was actually provided.
Example: Billing a complex surgical procedure (high-reimbursement DRG) when a simpler
         procedure was performed. Or billing 99215 (high complexity E/M) for a routine visit.
Indicators:
- Systematic shift to higher DRG weights over time without clinical justification
- E/M level distribution skewed heavily toward highest codes
- Outlier payments triggered consistently at single provider

B. PHANTOM BILLING
Definition: Billing for services never rendered to patients.
Indicators:
- Deceased beneficiaries appearing on claims after date of death
- Services billed while beneficiary was in another facility
- Beneficiary has no record of receiving service
- Claim submitted for future dates

C. UNBUNDLING
Definition: Billing separately for services that should be billed as one bundled procedure.
Example: Billing each component of a surgical package separately instead of the global fee.
Indicators:
- Multiple codes billed that CMS bundling edits prohibit
- Modifier 59 (distinct service) applied excessively to bypass edits

D. KICKBACKS AND REFERRAL FRAUD
Indicators:
- Unusual referral patterns: provider refers exclusively to one specialist
- Billing volume spikes after proximity to referring provider
- DME suppliers with no clinical relationship to ordering physicians

E. IDENTITY THEFT AND BILLING FRAUD
Indicators:
- Multiple providers billing for same beneficiary on same date
- Provider using expired or revoked NPI
- Claims submitted from addresses that are not medical facilities

2. STATISTICAL ANOMALY DETECTION THRESHOLDS

Charge-to-Payment Ratio Benchmarks:
- <3.0 : Normal range
- 3.0-6.0 : Acceptable, some variance expected
- 6.0-10.0 : Elevated, warrants monitoring
- 10.0-15.0 : High risk, manual review recommended
- >15.0 : Very high risk, immediate audit flag

Geographic Variance:
- Providers billing >200% of regional average for same service = audit flag
- Sudden geographic expansion of billing (new states/regions) = flag

Volume Anomalies:
- Provider billing same procedure >10x per day = flag
- Single provider billing >24 hours of time per day = impossible, automatic flag
- New provider billing at maximum level immediately = flag (no learning curve)

3. MEDICARE ERROR RATE TESTING (CERT) COMMON ERROR TYPES
- No documentation: Provider cannot produce medical records
- Insufficient documentation: Records don't support level billed
- Medically unnecessary: Service not reasonable/necessary per coverage rules
- Incorrect coding: Service coded does not match documentation
- Other billing errors: Wrong beneficiary, duplicate claim, non-covered service

4. RISK SCORING METHODOLOGY
Risk Score 0-100:
- 0-25: Low risk - routine processing
- 26-50: Moderate risk - secondary review recommended
- 51-75: High risk - pre-payment review required
- 76-100: Critical risk - immediate investigation, payment suspension

Key Risk Factors (weighted):
- Charge-to-payment ratio (25% weight)
- Deviation from peer benchmark (25% weight)
- Volume anomalies (20% weight)
- Historical error rate for provider (15% weight)
- Specialty/procedure mismatch (15% weight)
