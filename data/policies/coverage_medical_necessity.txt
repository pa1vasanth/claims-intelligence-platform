MEDICARE COVERAGE AND MEDICAL NECESSITY GUIDELINES

1. MEDICAL NECESSITY STANDARD
Medicare covers services that are "reasonable and necessary for the diagnosis or treatment of
illness or injury or to improve the functioning of a malformed body member."
(Social Security Act Section 1862(a)(1)(A))

Services NOT covered:
- Experimental or investigational treatments without approved clinical trial
- Cosmetic procedures unless medically required
- Routine physical examinations (with exceptions for Welcome to Medicare visit)
- Dental, vision, hearing aids (standard exclusions)
- Custodial care (not skilled care)

2. LOCAL COVERAGE DETERMINATIONS (LCDs)
Each Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC) publishes LCDs specifying:
- Which diagnosis codes support coverage for a specific procedure
- Frequency limitations (how often a service is covered)
- Documentation requirements specific to that service

Common LCD Requirements:
- Sleep studies (PSG/HSAT): Must have documented symptoms of sleep apnea (snoring, daytime
  sleepiness, witnessed apneas) before testing is covered
- Continuous glucose monitoring: Requires Type 1 or insulin-requiring Type 2 diabetes diagnosis
- Physical therapy: Requires documented functional limitation and expected improvement
- Imaging (MRI/CT): Must have clinical indication; not covered for routine screening

3. NATIONAL COVERAGE DETERMINATIONS (NCDs)
NCDs set national standards that override local policies:
- Implantable cardiac monitors: Covered for unexplained syncope or AF detection
- CAR-T therapy: Covered only at approved facilities with REMS program
- Lung cancer screening (LDCT): Ages 50-77, 20 pack-year smoking history, current or
  quit within 15 years, shared decision-making visit required

4. FREQUENCY AND QUANTITY LIMITS
Procedure-specific limits (examples):
- Colonoscopy (high risk): Every 2 years
- Colonoscopy (average risk): Every 10 years
- Mammography: Annually age 40+
- DEXA bone density scan: Every 2 years (more frequent if osteoporosis diagnosis)
- EKG: Covered for new patients or specific cardiac indications
- Vitamin B12 injections: Maximum 1 per month for pernicious anemia

Exceeding frequency limits without clinical justification = non-covered service.

5. INPATIENT ADMISSION CRITERIA (InterQual/Milliman)
For inpatient admission to be covered:
- Patient must require services that can only be provided in inpatient setting
- Condition must be expected to require 2+ midnight stays (Two-Midnight Rule)
- Observation status should be used for shorter stays
- Exceptions: Procedures on inpatient-only list (e.g., hip replacement, major cardiac surgery)

Two-Midnight Rule violations:
- Inpatient admission for single-day procedure covered under outpatient benefit
- Example: Cataract surgery billed as inpatient when should be outpatient
- Financial impact: Full inpatient DRG payment vs. APC outpatient payment

6. ADVANCE BENEFICIARY NOTICE (ABN)
When a service is likely not covered:
- Provider must issue ABN before delivering service
- Beneficiary must sign and choose to proceed at own expense
- Without ABN: Provider cannot bill beneficiary if Medicare denies
- Required for: Services expected to be denied as not medically necessary
